P1. How missing dates change market speed

P2. Working with a coding agent

I worked with a coding agent to extend the Campus Comps website from Part 1. I asked the agent to continue from that repository and put the first update on a branch I could open in GitHub. That made it easy to see what the agent had changed. A notebook would have made it easy to inspect a SQL query and its output together, but I would still have needed to turn the results into charts and make the website respond to each visitor's selections.

The price cutoff showed why I wanted a separate way to check the numbers. The Part 1 site used a minimum price of $1,000, and the final project introduced both a higher minimum and an upper limit. Davis still ranked first after that change. Looking only at the winner would have missed the fact that Chapel Hill's median moved from $735,000 to $734,000. The agent ran SQL against the original SQLite file to check every city as well as the record count. I prefer using a coding agent to build a site because it can connect the calculations, charts and controls in one iteration. A short SQL query is still my preferred way to trace an individual number back to the underlying sales.

Data & methods

Sale price scope

Q1 and most market questions use closed sales from $10,000 to $5,000,000, including both endpoints. Q2 uses all reported prices. Q3 compares both scopes. Q9 counts all transactions with a sale date for the chosen city and year. The explorer uses the same price scope as Q1.

Market time and missing values

Days on market is the number of calendar days from listing date to contract date. Both dates are required. A recorded same-day contract counts as zero days. Missing feature values are marked Unknown. A home with a missing numeric value is omitted when a related numeric filter is applied.

Locations, dates and keys

A city is the city and state together. A campus area follows the dataset's nearest university assignment. Year and month come from the closing date. Each row is a completed sale keyed by listing ID. Multiple sales at the same street address are retained.

Statistics and tax

The median is the middle value, averaging the two middle values for an even count. Q6 adds annual taxes and sale prices over the same eligible rows, then divides those totals. Annual tax must be positive and below 10% of the sale price.
